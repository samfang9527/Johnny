# Race-Aware Plans and Target Evidence

## Intent

An Active Projection should change its training purpose as its selected race approaches, include every planned race inside its date range, and express each running target with the best effort and pace evidence available. The deterministic Plan Skeleton owns dates, workout types, phase coverage, load, and race identity. The LLM may enrich coaching copy and valid step detail inside that skeleton, consistent with ADR-0001.

This extends the approved race-aware workload policy in `openspec/changes/2026-10-03-race-aware-workload-policy/design.md`. Its 56-day capacity snapshot, progression and cutback limits, injury and hard-session constraints, preparation-assessment semantics, version history, and pending coaching review remain in force. The new taper windows, race-effort RPE values, HR-reserve bands, and pace conversions below are versioned product heuristics requiring coaching review before release.

## Race strategy and workouts

- Keep the existing Race Strategy selection and 16-week projection horizon. The selected Race Goal remains the overall target; a distant target beyond the horizon does not cause an early taper.
- An explicitly chosen maintenance projection still displays and protects any race inside its four-week horizon, without treating one as the target or starting a target-race taper.
- Load all athlete-owned Race Goals with status `planned` or `active` whose dates fall within the projection, including `primary`, `secondary`, and `tune_up`. A selected target race inside the horizon is included exactly once. Exclude completed and cancelled goals.
- Every included goal becomes a distinct `race` workout on its event date, including a date outside preferred training days. Preserve the `race_goal_id`, name, priority, and distance as a snapshot on the workout. The plan's `race_id` identifies which of these is the target.
- A race workout has the event distance as planned distance and no invented planned duration. Its executable race step covers that distance. Its canonical display name uses the saved race name and distance. Race identity and load come from the trusted skeleton, never model text.
- A race replaces an ordinary run on the same date. Remove an optional ordinary run if adding an off-day race would exceed `max_runs_per_week`. Preserve every race if race count alone exceeds that limit, remove optional runs, and record a constrained-schedule factor.
- All races count as hard sessions for spacing and capacity. A selected target gets the full target-race taper. A non-target `primary` or `secondary` defaults to full performance effort (RPE 8). A `tune_up` defaults to controlled effort (RPE 6). The selected target is RPE 6 when its goal intent is `complete` and RPE 8 otherwise. These are advisory starting values, never a predicted finish time.
- Race dates remain protected when events are close or same-day. Same-day race workouts are distinct by Race Goal ID; no ordinary run occupies that date. Record a race-conflict factor rather than silently dropping a goal. Activity matching must not guess between same-day races.
- At plan generation and regeneration, supporting races adjust nearby scheduled training without changing the selected Race Strategy or restarting base/build/peak. Preserve already completed workouts and normal plan-version history.
- An Active Projection remains a versioned snapshot. If a Race Goal inside its remaining date range is added, moved, cancelled, or deleted after activation, show that the plan needs regeneration and use the existing manual regeneration flow with its current quota rules. No plan is silently changed. The race form must not claim the new event is already in the active plan.

## Phase and load policy

- Base, build, and peak prescribe distinct session mixes when quality is allowed. Base uses at most one controlled tempo or terrain-relevant hill session every second week; intervening weeks are aerobic. Build uses interval/hill for targets up to 10 km and tempo/hill for longer targets. Peak uses interval/tempo for targets up to 10 km and mostly tempo for longer targets; a terrain-focus week may substitute hill. The quality-workout count still obeys capacity, injury, spacing, and hard-session limits.
- A key Skeleton Workout carries an optional canonical `focus_zone` so a peak tempo can use a race-relevant zone rather than an unchanged threshold prescription. Use interval for short-race peak intervals, threshold for short-race tempo, and marathon for peak tempo at half-marathon distance and above. The LLM and deterministic templates must honor this focus while staying inside the prescribed duration. Keep the canonical zone with a target even when its primary kind becomes RPE.
- Start the target-race taper 14 days before races of at least 21,097 m and 7 days before shorter races, clipped to the projection start. The final three days before the target race contain no new quality session. Taper applies by workout date, including a week that crosses a phase boundary; taper workouts and long runs receive lower durations than the immediately preceding development load.
- A non-target performance race suppresses other hard sessions for 2 days before and after if shorter than 21,097 m, or 3 days before and 5 days after if longer. A controlled tune-up suppresses other hard sessions for 1 day before and after if shorter, or 2 days before and 3 days after if longer. In those windows, optional training is easy/recovery and at most 30 minutes. If windows overlap, use the stricter rule and retain every race. Races override an otherwise smaller hard-session limit; record the capacity conflict rather than removing the race.
- A race's distance is reported separately from prescribed training minutes. Do not force the race into a weekly minute budget or infer finish time from that budget. Calculate actual prescribed training minutes and preparation assessment after race and taper adjustments. Version the revised workload policy.
- Preserve the existing activity-derived capacity rules: use the full user-scoped 56-day running history for policy decisions, treat sparse history as low confidence, keep the approved 5% development ceiling, fourth-week cutback, long-run progression and caps, injury restrictions, and honest `gap`/`unknown` assessment. Supporting races do not turn an uncalibrated reference into `on_track`.

## Athlete data and target policy

- Add optional resting and maximum heart-rate fields to Athlete Settings. Accept a complete, valid pair with maximum greater than resting. Partial or invalid data never yields numeric HR zones. Preserve the current threshold-pace setting.
- For a valid pair, estimate HR zones with heart-rate reserve: `resting + fraction × (maximum - resting)`. V1 reserve bands are recovery 40–55%, easy 50–65%, steady 65–75%, marathon 70–80%, and threshold 80–90%. These are product estimates, not measured physiological thresholds. HR may be the primary target for sustained running; short intervals, hills, and race efforts remain RPE-led because an instantaneous HR number is a poor control for them.
- Without a valid HR pair, every running step uses RPE as its primary target. An independently supported estimated pace range may still accompany it. Rest and strength workouts have no running pace target.
- A running step may carry an estimated pace range regardless of whether its primary target is RPE, HR, or pace. Record pace source and confidence separately from target kind. Calculate ranges after model output and completion repair; do not accept model-supplied numeric pace or HR as authority.
- Threshold pace supplied in Athlete Settings is the strongest V1 pace anchor and retains the current zone factors. A suitable completed race result is a medium-confidence anchor only if an activity is matched to a completed race workout, its measured distance is within 5% of the event distance, and it is in the recent 56-day activity window.
- Select pace evidence in this order: explicit threshold-pace setting, qualifying completed race result, three qualifying paired easy activities, then selected target-race goal time. Never silently merge sources into a stronger confidence tier.
- Three or more comparable easy/long runs in the recent 28 days may provide a low-confidence anchor: each is matched to an easy or long-run workout, lasts at least 20 minutes, covers at least 3 km, gains no more than 10 m/km, and has average HR within the athlete's calculated easy range. Use their median pace as the easy-zone midpoint; derive other zones from the existing zone-factor midpoint ratios and widen the resulting ranges by 15%. A single ordinary run average is insufficient.
- A stated target-race finish goal may provide a low-confidence, explicitly goal-derived anchor. Map its distance to a reference zone (up to 5 km: interval; over 5 km through half marathon: threshold; over half through marathon: marathon; longer: easy), apply existing zone-factor midpoint ratios, and widen ranges by 20%. A qualifying completed race uses the same reference mapping with 10% widening. These are versioned V1 heuristics; neither goal pace nor weekly mileage is treated as a measured threshold pace.
- Pace zones for all running steps use a versioned mapping from the selected anchor and the step's effort zone. For RPE-only steps, map RPE 1–4 to easy, 5 to steady, 6 to marathon, 7 to threshold, 8 to interval, and 9–10 to repetition. When evidence is weaker, label the range as provisional. Hill/technical-terrain pace is a flat-ground reference, with effort governing execution. If no pace-bearing evidence exists, omit numeric pace.
- Saving a completed supporting race can improve future estimates only when an actual result is matched reliably to that race workout; its pre-race goal time remains goal-derived evidence.

## Generation, display, and safety

- The local gateway, LLM schema/prompt, validation, model repair, and deterministic completion templates all preserve race workouts and the same target policy. Incomplete or invalid model output cannot move, rename, omit, or resize races.
- The workout API and frontend show race name, distance, priority/target role, phase, primary effort, optional HR range, optional estimated pace range, and the pace estimate's source/confidence in Traditional Chinese. Existing historical workouts remain readable.
- The profile form validates and round-trips HR settings, including clearing either field. Numeric effort data is never fabricated to satisfy a display requirement.
- The primary target race still ends the race projection. Post-race recovery remains the existing Recovery Window.
